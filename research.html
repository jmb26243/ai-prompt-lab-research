UNDERSTANDING THE PROBLEM

Research & Findings

Exploring prompt engineering, AI experimentation, and how a structured learning tool could help students.

OUR RESEARCH QUESTION

How can a simple application help students experiment with AI prompts, evaluate the results, and learn which prompting approaches work better?

This question guides the research and development of AI Prompt Lab. Rather than assuming that one prompting technique is always best, the project explores how users can record, review, and compare their own experiments.

WHO ARE WE DESIGNING FOR?

Meet our target audience

AI Prompt Lab is designed for people who want to become more confident and effective when working with generative AI.

College students and beginner AI users

Our primary audience consists of college students and beginners who are learning to use generative AI for studying, research, brainstorming, and other everyday tasks.

These users may experiment with different prompts but have difficulty remembering which approaches worked well or understanding how changes to their instructions affected the AI's responses.

Design goal: Provide a structured, beginner-friendly workspace where users can save prompts, review AI responses, record evaluations, and learn from previous experiments.

BACKGROUND

Why study prompt engineering?

The way a user communicates with an AI model can influence the response it produces.

What is a prompt?

A prompt is an input given to an AI model to start a conversation or request a response. It can contain instructions, questions, context, or examples.

What is prompt engineering?

Prompt engineering is the process of designing and improving prompts to guide an AI model toward a desired response.

Key idea: prompting is iterative

A user can start with an initial prompt, review the response, and revise the instructions. This process can be repeated to explore different ways of communicating with an AI model.

Clear instructions matter

CRITICAL THINKING

Facts, interpretations & assumptions

It is important to distinguish documented information from our own conclusions and ideas.

FACT

What sources document

  • Prompts provide input and instructions to AI models.
  • Clear and specific instructions are recommended.
  • Reviewing and refining prompts is an established practice.
INTERPRETATION

What we conclude

  • Recording prompt attempts could make experimentation more organized.
  • Comparing results may help users recognize differences.
  • A history of experiments could support reflection and learning.
ASSUMPTION

What we still need to validate

  • Students want a dedicated place to save AI experiments.
  • Users will find a rating system helpful.
  • A private dashboard will encourage continued use.

FROM RESEARCH TO DESIGN

How research shapes AI Prompt Lab

Our findings help explain the features we plan to include in the application.

✎

Prompt and response records

Since prompting involves instructions and context, users should be able to save the original prompt alongside the AI response.

Design decision: Experiment creation form
◎

Ratings and personal notes

Users should be able to evaluate responses and record observations about what worked or what they would change.

Design decision: Evaluation fields
↗

Experiment history

Because prompt refinement is iterative, users should be able to revisit previous experiments and learn from earlier attempts.

Design decision: Private user dashboard

RESEARCH TRANSPARENCY

AI assistance and verification

AI tools supported the research and planning process.

How AI was used

ChatGPT was used to brainstorm project concepts, organize research findings, compare possible implementation approaches, and explore ideas for the application's design and architecture.

It also assisted with outlining the website content and explaining programming concepts relevant to the proposed project.

02

How information was verified

Technical recommendations and claims were compared with official documentation and original research sources whenever possible.

Project requirements were also checked against the IS218 assignment instructions. Suggestions that could not be independently confirmed were not presented as established facts.

03

Human decisions

The final project concept, intended audience, feature scope, and design decisions were reviewed and selected by the student.

AI-generated ideas were evaluated before being incorporated into the project.

04

Limitations of AI assistance

AI-generated information can contain errors, incomplete explanations, or recommendations that do not fit the project's needs.

Independent source checking, testing, and human judgment remain necessary throughout the development process.

LIMITATIONS & NEXT STEPS

What we still need to learn

The sources provide useful guidance, but they do not prove that AI Prompt Lab will improve student learning. That is an assumption we need to investigate.

A future step is to ask potential users about their experiences with AI prompts and show a peer the proposed workflow. Their feedback can help determine whether the planned features are understandable and useful.
